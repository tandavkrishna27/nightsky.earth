# Prepare a night-sky outing

Use the canonical workflow in `docs/product/workflows/prepare-outing.md`.

## Intent before tools

Default to outdoor observing; explicit hosted or accommodation requests override the default. Retain activity, origin, direction, exclusions, budget and corrections. Ask only the next decision-relevant missing detail. Apply the shared outdoor conversation contract in the workflow before the technical pipeline; distinguish provisional areas, exact sites and optional venues.

## First decision and answer rules

Before a candidate-site plan: obtain get_observing_location_details. If its actual timezone is unverified and the user has not explicitly named a display timezone, stop and ask one display-time-zone question. Wait for the reply; do not calculate a candidate or substitute city plan in this turn. A nearby city's timezone, the requested UTC window, or “use its timezone” is not a user choice of an unknown site timezone. Offer the base-city timezone as a question, never silently select it. If details are unavailable, explain that gap and ask whether the user wants a separately labelled named-city plan.

Do not give compass-direction setup advice in a planning answer. Say to use an on-site sky chart for orientation; a maximum-altitude sample is not a rising or session-wide bearing. Use the tool's answer-ready planning text for exact intervals and unknowns. Include the complete source links returned for every source used, rather than names alone or shortened URLs. Moon below the geometric horizon does not establish minimal moonlight; leave its observing impact unknown. Do not assert an uncalculated alternative night's visibility.

## Request and tool sequence

Confirm named destination, date/night, activity and relevant group or equipment needs. For “near me,” ask for town and country unless a location capability or approximate hint is actually exposed. If given coordinates, ask the user to name a destination and do not reverse-geocode or plan.

For a complete named-city plan, resolve_place then plan_observing_session provides the combined evidence; use focused tools only as needed, as specified below.

Give a concise checklist: current forecast and valid times, warm layers, water, charged phone, low-light use, return plan, and direct confirmation of public access, after-dark rules, permits and closures. Tailor activity gear; keep family suggestions general and do not claim a site is safe. If data is partial or unavailable, say so and never invent it. Do not promise an observation or photograph.

Candidate records describe mapped representative points with source-linked geometry. Access, darkness, safety and horizon remain unknown without affirmative evidence; preserve omissions, attribution and source vintage. Use the conditional discovery and candidate-planning flow below for exposed capabilities in the configured mode.

Meteor timing has 13 original NASA forecast maxima per year for 2025/2026 and 12 for 2027, with year-specific sources and no activity widths or annual recurrence. LEO has a NASA forecast maximum only in 2027; SSG/AND are omitted from that forecast, not proven absent. ## Interpret evidence and answer a beginner

When a plan contains darkness, explain civil (-6 degrees), nautical (-12 degrees) and astronomical (-18 degrees) intervals as nested periods below the Sun altitude thresholds, clipped to the requested session window. They are not exclusive twilight phases. Matching intervals never establish polar night or fake/mocked ephemerides: a short evening window can already lie entirely below all three thresholds. Describe darkness during that session; do not infer the whole day's solar behavior from it.

Treat target geometry as an observing opportunity. Milky Way visibility is conditional on dark skies, clouds, Moon, transparency and a clear local horizon; above-horizon core geometry alone cannot promise a visible band or photograph. Saturn may appear to the naked eye as a point; its rings require a telescope and suitable conditions. An interval's direction is sampled at maximum altitude among the evaluated points, not a bearing for the whole window. Keep returned sample times and intervals; use a cited sky chart for changing on-site orientation.

Keep missing weather and access unknown. Distinguish an enabled provider that is unavailable from an unconfigured fixture/artifact/index and a query outside configured-source coverage. Unsupported seeing/transparency, a full deep-sky catalog and global coverage are separate capability/data gaps; adding workflow text does not supply them. Report the returned reason and continue with supported evidence. State local permits or restrictions only with a returned or verified official source; otherwise ask the user to confirm with the land manager. General travel preparation is advice, not evidence of local rules or safe access.

## Final answer audit

Use this compact output procedure: mandatory factual core at most 120 words before Sources; keep the whole answer within 250 words including URLs when that maximum applies.
1. Core answer: begin with the outcome in one sentence, then at most three bullets with useful returned local times and timezone. Geometric opportunity is not best seeing. A clipped interval endpoint is not a sunset, sunrise or exclusive twilight-phase boundary.
2. Evidence: a few lines with each weather source's issuedAt, valid time/range, value and whether an instant sample or interval; state coverage and unknowns. When giving Moon phase or illumination, include “Moon impact unknown” even for Moon viewing. Moon-free or favourable conditions cannot follow from below-horizon geometry. Omit optional unrelated events and catalogue counts, especially when not asked; NASA timing counts and GMN model counts are independent.
3. Next step: give one practical next step or required clarification. If display timezone is unresolved, ask and wait. Do not invent later-night or next-night darkness comparisons. Ask for a new window without promising overlap; calculate it before giving times, visibility or comparative darkness. Remove compass setup, including parenthetical advice and pole-facing aurora advice. Only an explicit focused bearing question permits a returned azimuth with its exact sample time and returned sample basis; label a maximum-altitude sample as such.

Sources: for an aggregate plan, copy its complete Sources URL block from the answer-ready text; deduplicate exact URLs without ellipses instead of selecting backend source records. For focused tool answers, include relevant sources only, with complete returned URLs; candidate distance or access claims require the corresponding provider or catalogue sources. Negative or empty-event claims use sources too. Omit incidental empty-event sentences from non-event plans; supported in-window events may still be suggested with sources. If an event claim uses both years, include both year-specific URLs. Shorten advice before dropping a source; count all whitespace-separated words including Sources against the applicable total budget. Use URLs without long labels and trim optional advice, preserving mandatory safety.

For family preparation, use at most 150 words total including URLs: one 20–30 minute block within a returned opportunity, one practical preparation step, and optional age/gear clarification. Do not expand into a full inventory or claim that post-midnight is darker without calculated evidence. For unsupported or clarification responses, use at most 100 words total including URLs: one boundary sentence per request plus one question or next step. Wait for missing inputs before repeating a planning pipeline or fallback template.

When discussing solar safety, retain: Ordinary sunglasses are unsafe. Partial/annular viewing needs an undamaged ISO 12312-2 viewer; optical devices need a purpose-built filter secured over the front aperture. Eclipse glasses alone do not make telescope/binocular/camera viewing safe. Include the returned NASA safety URL.

Preserve returned geometry evidence with the selected snapshot: a bounding box, representative point, or reported region label alone does not prove administrative membership. If a requested spatial relation lacks point-specific evidence, keep the returned unsupported/partial reason rather than dropping the constraint. Historical climatology can be unavailable while current forecasts remain useful. Report its returned reason and requested coverage; never replace missing history with present weather, a global climate claim, or a fabricated clear-night percentage.
