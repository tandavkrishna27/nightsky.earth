## Finding

A compatible primary radiant/drift source exists. The exact author-deposited version of **Moorhead, Clements and Vida, Meteor shower radiant dispersions in Global Meteor Network data**, [arXiv 2205.15423v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15423v1), has an explicit [CC BY 4.0 license](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) link; the [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.15423v1) repeats that license. Use that identified version, rather than assuming publisher access implies reuse rights.

Its original fitted Table 1 covers all existing eight shower codes. It gives a reference solar longitude, Sun-centered geocentric ecliptic radiant longitude/latitude intercepts, linear slopes per degree of solar longitude, and uncertainties. Table 3 gives nominal fit-window lengths: PER 9, GEM 3, ORI 7, ETA 9, LYR 3, SDA 5, QUA 1, LEO 7 days. Observations span December 2018–May 2021. These windows constrain radiant modeling near the fit anchor; they are **not full activity seasons**. The paper explicitly says it did not measure shower duration after correcting observation gaps. Existing normalized activity dates therefore remain a separate source gate.

## Frame evidence and bounded retrieval

The GMN [archive index](https://globalmeteornetwork.org/data/traj_summary_data/) lists the complete archive as roughly 3 GB. It was **not downloaded**. Only its 449 KB [2018 trajectory summary](https://globalmeteornetwork.org/data/traj_summary_data/traj_summary_yearly_2018.txt) was read as a bounded primary reference. Its generated header was October 13, 2026 16:37:08 UTC. It provides UTC timestamp, solar longitude, J2000 geocentric RA/Dec/ecliptic values and separate apparent ground-fixed columns. One solar-longitude reference is 257.653525 degrees at 2018-12-10 01:06:56.588151 UTC. This is a source-format reference, not an independent universal positional accuracy test.

The provider's [WesternMeteorPyLib SolarLongitude implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wmpg/WesternMeteorPyLib/master/wmpl/Utils/SolarLongitude.py) documents its solar-longitude functions as J2000. Its JPL route derives longitude from heliocentric Earth position. The inference that the paper follows that same J2000 convention is supported by GMN's column documentation and implementation; implementation must compare against actual source rows before acceptance. Do not use Astronomy Engine's apparent ecliptic longitude of date without checking the precession/aberration distinction.

## Implemented evaluator and planner integration

Root reviewed the scope before implementation. Files: `data/events/meteor-radiants-gmn-2021.json`, `server/events/meteorRadiants.ts`, `tests/events/meteorRadiants.test.ts`. `evaluateRadiant(code, date, observer)` accepts the eight reviewed codes and returns a discriminated internal result. The planner evaluates one selected reviewed shower at session start, midpoint and end and preserves all three statuses in structured evidence. Shower names are not sent to the generic planet target tool. No numbers appear for outside-model/unsupported results. Available results retain source/license/vintage, fit anchor and signed offset, J2000 coordinates, geometric local altitude/azimuth, topocentric Moon separation/altitude, original parameter uncertainty, explicit frame labels and annual extrapolation flag.

The source observation interval is December 10, 2018–May 22, 2021, **not 2012–2021**. The prototype has an explicitly chosen engineering evaluation bound of 2018–2030; this is not a published scientific validity guarantee. Evaluating 2026 reuses the annual solar-longitude fit with extrapolation flagged. The reference solar longitude is a fit anchor, not an exact annual meteor peak. The nominal window conversion uses the paper's approximately 0.986 degrees/day; outside the reviewed offset domain, no radiant value is returned. The Earth heliocentric vector is rotated into fixed J2000 ecliptic coordinates and reversed for geometric solar longitude. It is not an apparent solar longitude of date.

The radiant is a geocentric mean direction transformed into the observer's frame, without zenith attraction, atmospheric refraction or terrain. Moon direction is topocentric equatorial of date, without refraction; its separation from the mean shower direction includes Moon parallax. These two direction conventions are stated in the result; it does not claim both are geocentric or that an individual meteor follows the mean.

TDD: the first run failed because the new module did not exist. Eight focused tests then passed; TypeScript passed. Tests cover all original coefficient transcriptions and uncertainties, independent raw GMN J2000 solar longitude (0.005 degree regression bound), before/at/after-anchor arithmetic for all eight, both sides of every nominal window, no outside-model numbers, wrapped longitude, independent classical precession plus spherical/GMST horizon geometry across six northern/southern/polar/dateline observers (0.08 degree engineering bound), and Moon separation using an alternative EQJ spherical-angle route plus observer-parallax response. These bounds are regression checks and do not establish real meteor visibility, full-season validity or model confidence intervals. The Moon test independently checks frame/angle wiring while still using the pinned astronomy engine for underlying lunar ephemeris.

## Further NASA 2019 activity-table audit

The accepted manuscript's Table 5 contains all eight shower codes, with separate peak/base components for Perseids and Leonids. Its text traces the combined table to Jenniskens (1994) naked-eye profiles and the authors' 2017 fits to fourteen years of CMOR observations; the table does not mark each row's originating source. It supplies activity-profile parameters rather than physical start/end dates. Numeric peak/reference longitudes also differ from the 2021 radiant fit anchors, so they must not be silently conflated.

NTRS's specific 2019 metadata says public use permitted, but does **not** label this mixed government/university/contractor-authored paper as wholly US-government work. Its arXiv version grants only nonexclusive repository distribution. NASA's [STI copyright guidance](https://sti.nasa.gov/disclaimers/), checked October 13, explains that privately contributed portions can retain separate copyrights, and government release permissions do not necessarily transfer. This does not prove Table 5 is prohibited or that physical numbers are copyrighted; it prevents treating the whole derived compilation as automatically public domain. Specific row provenance and permissible normalized reuse remain unresolved. No activity-profile table or old dates were imported under a fabricated license.

Even if that reuse review clears, turning exponential tails into an activity window requires a reviewed threshold; there is no unique natural beginning/end supplied by the table. Its radar-derived profiles cannot become personal visual counts without additional modeling. A full-season radiant fit or licensed time series remains needed to cover old activity windows beyond the short fitted domains. Thus this prototype prepares the requested altitude/Moon-separation path while preserving the explicit remaining full-journey gate.
