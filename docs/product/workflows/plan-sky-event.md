# Plan a sky event

## First decision and answer rules

Before a candidate-site plan: obtain get_observing_location_details. If its actual timezone is unverified and the user has not explicitly named a display timezone, stop and ask one display-time-zone question. Wait for the reply; do not calculate a candidate or substitute city plan in this turn. A nearby city's timezone, the requested UTC window, or “use its timezone” is not a user choice of an unknown site timezone. Offer the base-city timezone as a question, never silently select it. If details are unavailable, explain that gap and ask whether the user wants a separately labelled named-city plan.

Do not give compass-direction setup advice in a planning answer. Say to use an on-site sky chart for orientation; a maximum-altitude sample is not a rising or session-wide bearing. Use the tool's answer-ready planning text for exact intervals and unknowns. Include the complete source links returned for every source used, rather than names alone or shortened URLs. Moon below the geometric horizon does not establish minimal moonlight; leave its observing impact unknown. Do not assert an uncalculated alternative night's visibility.

## Goal and boundaries

Plan a meteor shower, eclipse, aurora attempt, conjunction, opposition, or another supported event. Event calendars, local geometry, weather and short-range aurora models have different coverage and must retain their own evidence and horizons.

## Event-specific limits

Meteor event search includes 13 original NASA forecast maxima per year in 2025/2026 and 12 in 2027, from NTRS 20240012381, 20250009524 and 20260009360. Preserve each returned year-specific source/vintage and exact peak/local conversion. The 2027 table labels UT to minute precision; ISO Z normalization is not independently established subminute UTC accuracy. LEO has a forecast maximum only in 2027; its enhanced maximum is not a generic annual Leonid date. SSG and AND omission in that report does not establish physical absence. LYR, ORI, NTA, STA and HYD have no retained maxima. Peaks are instants, not full seasons, annual recurrence or personal rates. Unsupported years and empty results never establish absence of activity.

Radiant calculations are a separate source covering PER, GEM, ORI, ETA, LYR, SDA, QUA, LEO, CAP, NTA, STA and HYD near their reference solar longitudes. QUA, ETA, SDA, PER, CAP, GEM and (only in 2027) LEO overlap the NASA timing lists. NASA-only codes have unknown radiants; do not substitute another shower or infer visibility from a peak. Pass one selected shower name/code as the meteor planner target and preserve unavailable or unsupported results. Read `evidence.meteorRadiantSamples`: the planner evaluates session start, midpoint and end, returning altitude, azimuth and Moon separation only inside the nominal fit domain. Preserve evaluation times, source/license/vintage, fit offset, frame and annual extrapolation flag. Out-of-model samples contain no substituted numbers. Three point samples do not establish continuous visibility, full-season activity, rates or exact peaks. A reference solar longitude is a fit anchor, not an annual peak instant. Observations span 2018-12-10 through 2021-05-22; annual reuse inside the engineering 2018–2030 evaluator range is flagged as extrapolation outside those observations. For eclipses, state local visibility and safety limits; for solar viewing retain the returned NASA safety source: ordinary sunglasses are unsafe, partial/annular viewing needs an undamaged ISO 12312-2 viewer, and optical devices need a purpose-built filter secured over the front aperture. Eclipse glasses alone do not make telescope/binocular/camera viewing safe. Aurora outlooks are not long-range predictions or visibility guarantees. If an event class is unsupported, say so and offer only the evidence that is available.

## Calculated planet event coverage

`search_sky_events` now includes outer-planet oppositions and explicitly labeled inferior/superior solar alignments from relative heliocentric ecliptic longitude, ignoring ecliptic latitude, in the bounded engineering range 1900–2100. These are global alignment times. Every planet alignment has local visibility not-evaluated; use `get_target_visibility`, darkness and available forecast for the selected named destination before suggesting a night. Solar alignment is not an observing recommendation; never suggest unfiltered solar viewing. Do not relabel a solar alignment as an apparent planet-pair conjunction, minimum angular separation or closest-distance instant. The latter remain unsupported; conjunction coverage is partial. Preserve truncation, out-of-horizon and missing-source states. Independent 2026 comparisons are coarse event-identity checks between different definitions, not an all-years accuracy guarantee.

`greatest-elongation` separately covers Mercury and Venus using the maximum geocentric three-dimensional angular distance from the Sun, with light-time and aberration corrections, within the same engineering range. Preserve the returned body, angle and morning/evening geometry. Evening means east of the Sun and morning means west; neither establishes a local observing window, horizon clearance, darkness or visual detectability. Local visibility stays not-evaluated. For a named-place session, check the selected planet with `get_target_visibility`, darkness and available weather before suggesting a time. Never suggest unfiltered solar viewing. Three coarse 2026 reference comparisons cover Mercury morning/evening and Venus evening; they are finite regressions, not an all-years accuracy guarantee or a verified Venus-morning time/angle reference.

## Interpret evidence and answer a beginner

When a plan contains darkness, explain civil (-6 degrees), nautical (-12 degrees) and astronomical (-18 degrees) intervals as nested periods below the Sun altitude thresholds, clipped to the requested session window. They are not exclusive twilight phases. Matching intervals never establish polar night or fake/mocked ephemerides: a short evening window can already lie entirely below all three thresholds. Describe darkness during that session; do not infer the whole day's solar behavior from it.

Treat target geometry as an observing opportunity. Milky Way visibility is conditional on dark skies, clouds, Moon, transparency and a clear local horizon; above-horizon core geometry alone cannot promise a visible band or photograph. Saturn may appear to the naked eye as a point; its rings require a telescope and suitable conditions. An interval's direction is sampled at maximum altitude among the evaluated points, not a bearing for the whole window. Keep returned sample times and intervals; use a cited sky chart for changing on-site orientation.

Keep missing weather and access unknown. Distinguish an enabled provider that is unavailable from an unconfigured fixture/artifact/index and a query outside installed coverage. Unsupported seeing/transparency, a full deep-sky catalog and global coverage are separate capability/data gaps; adding workflow text does not supply them. Report the returned reason and continue with supported evidence. State local permits or restrictions only with a returned or verified official source; otherwise ask the user to confirm with the land manager. General travel preparation is advice, not evidence of local rules or safe access.

## Moon timing and sampled directions

Do not infer moonrise, moonset or a moonless window from illumination or sampled altitude. Low Moon altitude does not establish negligible moonlight. Report illumination and sampled altitude as calculated values; Moon timing requires explicit supported rise/set or below-horizon interval evidence. Keep light pollution, transparency and the local horizon unknown unless supplied. An azimuth sampled at maximum altitude is not a rise-time or whole-session bearing. Say no supported event was returned rather than no event occurs when calendar coverage is incomplete. Preserve relevant returned source links.
