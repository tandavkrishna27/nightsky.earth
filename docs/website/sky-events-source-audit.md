# Sky Events source audit — expanded major-event catalogue

Reviewed 2026-10-16. Current scope: October 2026–September 2027. Supersedes the initial 54-entry selected calendar. Current 140 occurrences: 50 principal Moon phases, 13 showers, 5 eclipses, 25 planetary milestones, 41 close-pairing/occultation references and 6 Earth seasonal/orbital markers.

## Source and completeness policy

All four principal lunar phases, solar/lunar eclipses, outer-planet oppositions, inner-planet greatest elongations, solar conjunctions, four seasonal turning points and Earth perihelion/aphelion in the year-specific Fred Espenak UTC almanacs are included. Every almanac occultation flag is included. Other Moon pairings qualify within 1 degree; other bright-object pairings qualify within 2 degrees of the source reference. Fainter/minor showers, ordinary monthly lunar nodes/perigees, distant pairings and other planets’ perihelia/aphelia are outside this major-event policy. Aurora, new comet discoveries and satellite passes need current or local evidence and are not invented as fixed global dates.

All 127 qualifying non-meteor source rows matched by independent raw-HTML parsing. Twelve AMS in-window shower entries plus October Draconids produce thirteen showers. The evidence fixture is tests/fixtures/major-event-evidence.json; the build-required IDs and public policy are shared/skyEventCoverage.ts. These freeze the audited scope and detect accidental removal. They are not a claim of a live exhaustive feed.

## Precision and corrections

Sky Event Almanacs Courtesy of Fred Espenak, www.AstroPixels.com. Author explicitly equates GMT with UTC. Minute-valued references remain UTC instants converted by named zone. Hour-only predictions remain approximate source dates. A lunar pairing’s almanac reference is not a local occultation contact time or guaranteed local closest approach. Seasonal names are hemisphere-neutral.

NASA solar/lunar tables and 2027 annual overview cross-check eclipse date/type and geographic meaning. Their TD clock values are NOT copied into UTC fields. Eclipse Predictions by Fred Espenak, NASA’s GSFC. July18’s extremely slight penumbral encounter remains clearly distinguished from a dramatic visible eclipse.

Meteor observing-night ranges use https://www.amsmeteors.org/calendar/ and NASA’s individual shower material/current shower index. Source-night dates do not get converted to invented local-midnight appointments. The initial coarse annual almanac remains listed where useful as a secondary reference. Existing public IDs are preserved, even when their embedded original reference date differs from the revised observing-night range.

Leonids: revised to Nov16–17,2026 (AMS and NASA) from the old coarse Nov18 almanac marker. Southern Delta Aquariids: revised to Jul30–31,2027 (AMS) from Jul28. Lyrids: AMS Apr21–22, NASA Apr22–23, almanac Apr23; bracket Apr21–23 and explain the disagreement. Alpha Capricornids: peak Jul30–31; AMS activity header Jul3–Aug15 conflicts with narrative Jul12–Aug12, so no unqualified exact activity interval. Draconids: Oct9 from indexed primary IMO2026 extract, with activityOct6–10; full PDF retrieval was unavailable, and no claim of full-document review is made. It remains an approximate UT-source date, not a precise UTC time.

Meteor activity periods and Moon-phase context are explained in guides. Only peak references are indexed in the calendar to avoid weeks of repeated activity rows. No numerical personal meteor-rate guarantee, computed local Moon illumination or weather prediction is asserted.
