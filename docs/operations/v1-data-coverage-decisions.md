For CC BY choices, preserve authors, exact work/version, source and license links, supplied notices, and a description of transcription/model changes; avoid implying endorsement or adding restrictions to licensed material. The [CC BY 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en) governs, rather than this summary. A license cannot establish scientific validity or grant rights the licensor does not own.

The feasible next research path is source-by-source acceptance of original licensed scientific results, beginning with the two-shower A&A intervals and a provenance audit of other original activity models. This can be done through bounded publication reading without historical archives. A season definition must distinguish a reported typical interval, half-maximum width and an engineering threshold: a decaying profile has no unique physical start/end. Converting J2000 solar-longitude ranges into a requested year's UTC dates requires matching frame/convention and boundary validation. Annual intervals must be labeled typical recurrence, with unsupported outbursts and year-specific rates explicit. Missing full-season radiants must stay unavailable even when a season interval is available. Search has not established one free, explicitly licensed, scientifically complete source for all requested seasons and radiants; that is an evidence gap, not proof no such source exists.

## Global OSM coverage with local artifacts and no custom database

Global named-place resolution and coordinate-based MET planning are different coverage claims from observing-site candidates. GeoNames resolves named places and MET can be requested for resolved coordinates; neither populates a worldwide candidate inventory. Existing MET reference probes do not measure every global location. Do not label a globally callable planner as globally complete candidate coverage.

The present regional architecture is useful for the intended global path: build selected candidate points offline, partition dense regions, preserve object identity and provenance, and serve immutable local artifacts on demand. It does not yet establish that worldwide output fits its caps. [Overpass's primary manual](https://dev.overpass-api.de/overpass-doc/en/preface/commons.html) calls worldwide box stitching and use of public instances as an application backend problematic. Global expansion therefore needs a separately budgeted offline extract build, not runtime Overpass or a global public-instance scrape. [Geofabrik technical documentation](https://download.geofabrik.de/technical.html) describes regional extracts and update files; [OSM's license page](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) specifies ODbL and attribution. Before distribution, decide the derived database obligations and provide the applicable database/version offer; embedded source credit alone is insufficient.

A feasible no-database global strategy uses a larger, temporary/offline build environment and a small serving host. Process one reviewed extract at a time under external wall-time/RSS/disk limits; partition output using the selected node/way point policy and resolve overlaps by OSM identity before acceptance. Keep complete per-partition provenance and a coverage ledger including gaps. Preserve snapshot vintages across updates rather than retimestamping old rows. Publish a new immutable release and pin its manifest digest, validate all artifacts, then switch and retain the previous release for rollback. Choose a periodic full rebuild or an offline incremental process after measuring source, scratch and update costs; this packet sets no unmeasured cadence.

Worldwide candidate count, serialized size, geographic distribution, boundary duplication, build duration, peak memory, complete artifact validation cost, update cost and selected-host concurrency remain unmeasured. If the reviewed global output exceeds 32 regions/100,000 rows, the current provider cannot serve it as one global manifest. A future measured catalog design/cap change requires approval and implementation; many independent manifests cannot be assumed to work today. Relation or polygon support is optional unless the original requirements demand it. Representative points with explicit geometry/access limits can serve candidate discovery; inventing polygon work would not resolve missing worldwide source coverage.

## Decisions requiring explicit acceptance

1. **Meteor source method:** accept the original NASA maxima and original CC BY fits as bounded existing support; choose whether to pursue original licensed typical-season results through bounded research. Accepting this route does not close full-season coverage or authorize bulk data.
2. **Meteor completeness:** decide the intended season and year scope and how unsupported codes/dates are presented. Peak-only or two-shower seasons are reduced scope and must be explicitly accepted if used for V1; they cannot silently replace the requested meteor journey.
3. **Global candidate build:** approve the selected tags, point/geometry policy, geographic coverage target, ODbL distribution plan and separate offline build budget. Establish complete measured output before deciding whether current manifest caps suffice. Free sources do not make infrastructure free.
4. **V1 scope:** retain the global/free objective as unresolved until its required candidate/event evidence exists, or explicitly accept a public product with named partial coverage and limitations. This document makes no such scope decision on the owner's behalf.

Historical climate downloads remain deferred. Existing three-place 2025 samples and fictional multi-year format fixtures cannot become global 30-year climate coverage through a source or scope label. This dependency is retained rather than expanded into new work. The packet changes no status register, runtime contract, CI gate or release claim.
