# Sky Events source audit — expanded major-event catalogue

Reviewed 2026-10-18. Current scope: October 2026–September 2027. Supersedes the initial 54-entry selected calendar. Current 135 occurrences: 50 principal Moon phases, 8 meteor maxima, 5 eclipses, 25 planetary milestones, 41 close-pairing/occultation references and 6 Earth seasonal/orbital markers.

## Source and completeness policy

All four principal lunar phases, solar/lunar eclipses, outer-planet oppositions, inner-planet greatest elongations, solar conjunctions, four seasonal turning points and Earth perihelion/aphelion in the year-specific Fred Espenak UTC almanacs are included. Every almanac occultation flag is included. Other Moon pairings qualify within 1 degree; other bright-object pairings qualify within 2 degrees of the source reference. Fainter/minor showers, ordinary monthly lunar nodes/perigees, distant pairings and other planets’ perihelia/aphelia are outside this major-event policy. Aurora, new comet discoveries and satellite passes need current or local evidence and are not invented as fixed global dates.

All 127 qualifying non-meteor source rows matched by independent raw-HTML parsing. The meteor calendar includes only eight 2026–2027 nighttime-capable maxima transcribed from year-specific NASA MEO Table 3 reports. The evidence fixture is tests/fixtures/major-event-evidence.json; build-required IDs and public policy are shared/skyEventCoverage.ts. These freeze the audited scope and detect accidental removal. They are not a claim of a live exhaustive feed.

## Precision and corrections

AstroPixels grants reproduction of its almanac data when accompanied by the exact acknowledgment “Sky Event Almanac Courtesy of Fred Espenak www.AstroPixels.com”. The Sky Events page and event details display that acknowledgment. The permission applies to the data, not AstroPixels photographs, prose or page design. The author explicitly equates GMT with UTC. Minute-valued references remain UTC instants converted by named zone. Hour-only predictions remain approximate source dates. A lunar pairing’s almanac reference is not a local occultation contact time or guaranteed local closest approach. Seasonal names are hemisphere-neutral.

NASA solar/lunar tables and 2027 annual overview cross-check eclipse date/type and geographic meaning. Their TD clock values are NOT copied into UTC fields. Eclipse Predictions by Fred Espenak, NASA’s GSFC. July18’s extremely slight penumbral encounter remains clearly distinguished from a dramatic visible eclipse.

Meteor maxima use the year-specific NASA Meteoroid Environment Office (MEO) forecast reports, Table 3. Only the eight night-capable 2026–2027 entries with admitted source evidence are listed. Their UT model maxima are represented as ISO UTC instants at the report’s minute precision; minute precision does not imply subminute accuracy or a guaranteed local observing opportunity. NASA’s annual outlook reports maxima, not complete activity seasons, local visibility, or personal rates. No AMS calendar dates are reproduced. The source report and table are credited on the event details. Existing public event IDs remain stable for links, even where their original date suffix differs from the revised peak instant.

NASA MEO report reference: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250009524 (2026) and https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20260009360 (2027). Only peak references are indexed. No activity-season width, numerical personal meteor-rate guarantee, computed local Moon illumination or weather prediction is asserted.
