## Proposed representation and honest coverage

Keep ERA5 final `total_cloud_cover`, supplied quarter-degree nodes, and the current nearest-cell method as the reference. Keep whole-surface coverage, including oceans, islands and poles, as the eventual global target. Partition the source lattice into deterministic rectangles with unique integer-index ownership; normalize the longitude seam once. Retain actual coordinates and requested versus returned coordinate inventories. Pole and longitude endpoint conventions require source admission; a guessed cell count must not become the release inventory.

Build a sparse first release from admitted named destinations worldwide, deduplicating their nearest quarter-degree source nodes. This is a feasible incremental route under the present 10,000 cell/IANA-zone pair ceiling. Its promise must read “historical context at explicitly covered cells and zones”; all other queries return not-covered. Destinations distributed across continents do not establish complete geographic coverage. Publish country/continent coverage, exact cells/pairs, mapping distances and a residual uncovered inventory. No interpolation, distant-cell fallback, ocean omission or coarse lattice may quietly stand in for the promised complete quarter-degree baseline. Land masks or a coarser global lattice are separate owner decisions with changed claims and mapping error.

The complete grid cannot fit the present runtime contract even with one zone per cell: 1,038,240 rectangular nodes versus 10,000 admitted pairs. Full coverage needs a reviewed increase or hierarchical release/coverage architecture and measured host admission before its output can be installed. Building source files alone does not solve this serving gate.

### Explicit timezone strategy

Freeze mappings with the admitted gazetteer edition, timezone database version, coordinates, cell IDs, nearest-cell distance/tie rules and mapping provenance. Use each named record's explicit IANA zone. A shared physical cell can produce several aggregates for different admitted zones; source acquisition deduplicates the cell while computation/publication count every distinct cell/zone pair. Record the destination coordinate and the source-cell coordinate separately: solar darkness is evaluated at the source cell under the existing method.

A named-record timezone does not establish a geographic timezone polygon for arbitrary points. For arbitrary coordinates require an explicitly supplied supported IANA zone and label its geographic suitability unverified; serve only an existing exact pair. Otherwise return unavailable historical local-night context. Do not infer a zone from longitude, country, nearest city or a display setting. Complete global civil-time coverage needs a separately admitted polygon/assignment source, edition, rights, ocean/polar policy, border uncertainty and historical-rule handling. Pin IANA rules for historical DST/date-line changes; flag uncertain border mappings and permit explicit user correction. A UTC context product could be reviewed separately but cannot inherit the existing local-night claim.

## Frozen statistic and source requirements

The baseline observing nights are exactly 1991-01-01 through 2020-12-31, local noon to next local noon. Bins are observing-night month × **UTC hour**, 288 bins; they are not local clock-hour bins. Retain Astronomy Engine 2.1.19, solar altitude <= -18 degrees and inclusive cloud thresholds 0.1/0.25/0.5. Generate expected eligible hours independently of source rows. Missing or invalid observations stay missing, without interpolation or zero fill. Preserve >=90% eligible completeness, >=27 qualifying represented years, and existing selected-bin-year and month-year 80% completeness guards. No astronomical darkness is its own state.

There are exactly 10,958 days / 262,992 hours in the baseline. The existing four UTC outer padding days add 96 hours, giving **263,088 source hours per cell**. Source range is 1990-12-30T00:00Z inclusive to 2021-01-03T00:00Z exclusive. Internal boundaries share neighboring monthly partitions. Preserve the 48-hour displacement guard and stop if it cannot support a zone's night assignment. Baseline labels require the full period and quality gates; a full-period envelope with insufficient observations still fails qualification. Historical sample frequencies are neither on-demand forecasts nor predictions of an entire clear night.
